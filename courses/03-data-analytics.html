Data Analytics - turning stored data into answers you can trust

Kasa now has a warehouse (Session 2). But a warehouse full of data is not the same as a business that trusts its numbers. This is the analytics tier: eleven products that take stored data and turn it into answers people can stake a decision on. It is the biggest session in the course because analytics is where most companies live - and where most of them quietly go wrong. We group the eleven into a simple arc: first you trust the number, then you show the number, then you run the whole business on it.

Part 0

Where analytics sits on the ladder

Infrastructure answered "where does our data live?" Analytics answers the next question every leader actually cares about: "what happened, and can I trust the number?" This is the teal tier. It sits on top of the warehouse and under data science - you cannot predict the future until you can measure the present without arguing about it. Kasa arrives here with data finally in one place but with three teams still quoting three different revenue figures. The eleven products in this session fix that, in order: trust the number (validate it, define it, structure it, score the definitions), show the number (report, dashboard, scorecard, self-serve), then run on the number (investigate, monitor, share the story).

Part 1 · trust the number

Before you show anything, earn the number 9 min live

A beautiful dashboard on an unvalidated, undefined number is a confident lie - it just makes the wrong answer look official. So the analytics tier starts underground, with the unglamorous work nobody applauds: check the data is real, define what each metric means, structure the metrics into a tree, then score the definitions so they do not rot. Get this wrong and everything above it inherits the error. These four products are Kasa's foundation for trust.

LiveData Validation2 min▶

What it is. Automated checks that confirm data is complete, valid, and fresh before anyone is allowed to use it. It is the gate at the front door of every report.

Kasa exampleA rule rejects a store's nightly load if its row count drops more than 30% versus the trailing average - the load is quarantined, not silently averaged into the regional total.
Business valueNo garbage ever reaches a dashboard. The number people see is the number that was actually true.
Reach for it whenBefore ANY reporting. This is the gate - build it first, or everything downstream inherits bad data.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Real world

Kasa's Malaysia revenue looked flat for three weeks - not great, but not alarming. Then Raj added row-count and freshness validation and found one busy KL store had been silently half-loading every night for a month: the ETL timed out, wrote what it had, and moved on. The "flat" number was actually a hidden decline masked by missing rows. Validation did not just clean the data - it uncovered a real business problem the polished dashboards had been hiding. That is why the gate comes before the display.

LiveMetric Development2 min▶

What it is. Defining a metric rigorously - its formula, its grain, its filters, and its edge cases - so that everyone who computes it gets the same answer.

Kasa exampleNail "conversion rate" once: which sessions count, which orders count, does an app-then-store purchase count, what about returns inside the window. One definition, written down.
Business valueEveryone computes the metric the same way, so the number stops changing depending on who pulled it.
Reach for it whenA term means three different things to three different teams and every meeting starts by reconciling definitions.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
LiveKPI System Design2 min▶

What it is. A coherent tree of KPIs linked to goals and to owners - a hierarchy, not a random pile of two hundred metrics.

Kasa exampleA north-star metric (net revenue retention) breaks down into input KPIs - basket size, visit frequency, active customers - each with a named owner who moves it.
Business valueMetrics that drive action rather than vanity. Every number ladders up to a goal and down to a person.
Reach for it whenYou have two hundred metrics and no hierarchy - nobody can say which ones actually matter.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Self-studyData Dictionary Scorecard2 min read▶

What it is. A health score on the data dictionary itself - the percentage of fields that are defined, owned, and fresh. It measures whether your definitions are actually maintained.

Kasa exampleA tracked number: "78% of gold-layer tables documented, 62% with a named owner" - reported monthly, trending up as governance work lands.
Business valueMakes governance measurable and therefore fundable. You cannot get budget for "better definitions"; you can get budget to move 78% to 95%.
Reach for it whenThe dictionary exists but rots - fields drift, owners leave, and nobody notices until a report breaks.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Why this closes Part 1 Validation, metric development and KPI design build trust once. The dictionary scorecard is what keeps that trust from decaying - it turns "we defined things last year" into a living number the CDAO reviews every month.
Part 2 · show the number

Now put the trusted number in front of people 8 min live

Once a number is trustworthy, the job is to get it to the right person in the right form. That is not one product - it is four, each for a different audience and rhythm. Smart reporting replaces the hand-built weekly deck. Dashboarding gives a live view to watch. Executive scorecards compress everything to the few numbers a board cares about. Self-serve BI lets business users answer their own questions without a ticket. Match the product to the audience, or you will build a board scorecard for an analyst and a raw dashboard for a CEO.

LiveSmart Reporting2 min▶

What it is. Reports that are automated, refreshed on a schedule, and self-explaining - not hand-built decks somebody rebuilds every week.

Kasa exampleThe weekly store-performance report generates itself every Monday at 6am, pulls validated numbers, writes its own summary, and lands in inboxes before anyone arrives.
Business valueHours given back and copy-paste errors removed. The analyst stops being a human ETL pipeline.
Reach for it whenSomeone rebuilds the same report by hand on a schedule - the clearest "heroic one-off" waiting to become a product.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
LiveDashboarding2 min▶

What it is. Live visual views of metrics built for monitoring and exploration - the thing people leave open on a second monitor.

Kasa exampleThe daily sales and foot-traffic dashboard, filterable by region and store, refreshed hourly so store ops can see today unfold.
Business valueEveryone sees the same live picture. The debate moves from "whose number" to "what do we do about it."
Reach for it whenA metric needs continuous watching rather than a one-off answer.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Real world

Kasa's first instinct was the classic mistake: forty dashboards, one per team, all built fast on unvalidated data. Within a month the Monday leadership meeting had become a ritual argument - finance's dashboard said revenue was up, ops' said flat, marketing's said down, and everyone trusted their own. Sofia killed thirty of the forty, pointed the survivors at the validated warehouse marts, and made one shared regional dashboard the single source. The lesson Kasa learned the expensive way: a dashboard is only worth building after the number under it is trusted. Display without trust just multiplies the argument.

LiveExecutive Scorecard2 min▶

What it is. The few board-level numbers, each shown against its target, on a single page. Not a dashboard to explore - a verdict to read.

Kasa exampleThe CFO's one-pager: revenue, gross margin, NPS and cash runway, each versus target with a direction arrow (the mock above).
Business valueLeadership aligned on what matters. The meeting spends its time on the one red number, not on reconciling four green ones.
Reach for it whenExecs ask "how are WE doing?" every week and get a different-looking answer each time.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Real world

Before the scorecard, Kasa's monthly board pack was 42 slides and the board still left unsure whether the quarter was good. Mei replaced the opening with a single validated one-pager - four KPIs, each against target, margin flagged red. The first meeting using it lasted twenty minutes less and produced an actual decision: pause a low-margin promo. The scorecard did not add information; it removed all the information that was not a decision. That subtraction is why it sits at the top of the trust pyramid - it only works because every number on it was validated, defined, and laddered up from the KPI tree beneath.

Self-studySelf-Serve BI Enablement2 min read▶

What it is. The tools, training, and governed datasets that let business users answer their own questions without filing a ticket to the analytics team.

Kasa exampleStore managers slice their own store's numbers - by day, by category, by staff shift - from a governed, certified dataset, with no request queue.
Business valueAnalysts are freed from the ad-hoc queue and get back to real analysis. Decisions happen at the edge, faster.
Reach for it whenThe analytics team has become a bottleneck - every question waits in line behind the last one.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Self-serve needs a floor, not just a tool Handing people a BI tool on ungoverned data recreates the forty-dashboard mess at scale. Self-serve only works on top of Part 1 - certified datasets and shared metric definitions - otherwise everyone self-serves a slightly different wrong number.
Part 3 · run on the number

From having numbers to running on them 6 min live

Trusted, visible numbers are still inert until the organization actually operates on them. The last three products turn analytics into a habit: ad-hoc thematic analysis answers the novel question no dashboard covers, business performance monitoring tells you when something drifts so you do not have to keep looking, and regular insights sharing turns data into narrative the org acts on. This is where a company stops "having data" and starts being run by it.

LiveAd-hoc Thematic Analysis2 min▶

What it is. A deep, one-off study that answers a specific, high-stakes business question no standing dashboard was built to cover.

Kasa example"Why did Malaysia gross margin drop in Q2?" - a focused investigation across mix, promos, freight and returns that lands a cause and a recommendation in a week.
Business valueA decision, fast, when the dashboards cannot answer it - the analyst earning their keep on judgment, not plumbing.
Reach for it whenA novel, high-stakes question appears that no existing report was designed to answer.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
LiveBiz Performance Monitoring2 min▶

What it is. Ongoing tracking of business health with alerting on drift - the system tells you when something moves, so you do not have to go look.

Kasa exampleAn alert fires when a region's average basket size falls two weeks running, routed straight to that region's owner before it shows up in the monthly review.
Business valueProblems get caught while they are still small and cheap to fix, instead of surfacing a quarter late.
Reach for it whenYou need to be told when something changes, not to remember to check a dashboard.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
Self-studyRegular Insights Sharing2 min read▶

What it is. A cadenced ritual that turns data into narrative for the organization - a recurring moment where numbers become a story people act on.

Kasa exampleSofia's monthly "three things the data says" note - short, opinionated, one chart each, sent to the whole leadership team and referenced in the next planning cycle.
Business valueA data culture rather than just data infrastructure. The numbers change behavior because someone tells their story on a rhythm.
Reach for it whenThe dashboards exist and are trusted, but nobody actually acts on them.
Tier🟡 Data Analytics
This is the product most orgs skip It has no software to buy, so it gets forgotten - and then leaders wonder why their expensive dashboards changed nothing. Insight without a ritual to deliver it stays a screen no one opens. The cadence is the product.
Before Session 4

This week ◐ 20 min total

  • Audit one number. Pick a metric your org quotes constantly - revenue, conversion, active users. Ask two teams how they compute it. If the answers differ, you have found a metric-development gap.
  • Find your validation gate. Is there anything stopping bad data reaching your dashboards, or does it flow straight through? If there is no gate, that is your first build.
  • Count your dashboards. How many exist, and how many does anyone actually act on? The ratio tells you whether you have a display problem or a "run on the number" problem.
  • Optional: sketch your own one-page executive scorecard - the four numbers you would show a board, each against a target. If you cannot fill four, you have a KPI-system gap.
Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why does data validation come before dashboarding?

Validation is the gate at the front door. Display without trust just multiplies the argument - Kasa learned this by killing thirty of forty dashboards built on ungoverned data.

2 · A dashboard and an executive scorecard differ mainly in that...

A scorecard removes everything that is not a decision. It sits at the top of the trust pyramid because every number on it was validated, defined, and laddered up from the KPI tree.

3 · The "trust pyramid" says an executive scorecard is only as honest as...

Higher rests on lower: the scorecard sits on the KPI tree, which sits on metric definitions, which sit on validation. Weaken any layer and the top becomes a polished wrong answer.
