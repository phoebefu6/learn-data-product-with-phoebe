Part 1 · the definition

A data product is not a chart 6 min live

A one-off chart answers a question once and dies in a slide. A data product is built, owned, and maintained - it delivers the same value again and again, for many users, without someone rebuilding it by hand. That single word, maintained, is what separates a real product from a clever notebook. It is also why every product in this course has an owner, a refresh, and a way to break.

LiveThe four words that make it a product3 min▶

Every product in this catalog passes the same four tests. If a thing fails them, it is a nice analysis, not a product - and it will not survive contact with a real org.

  • Built - engineered as a repeatable system, not assembled by hand each time.
  • Owned - a named person or team is responsible when it breaks or drifts.
  • Maintained - it refreshes, it is monitored, and it can be fixed.
  • Valuable + repeatable - it serves the same need over and over for real users.
Real world

Kasa's finance analyst rebuilt the "regional revenue" spreadsheet every Monday for a year - copy, paste, reconcile, email. That was a heroic one-off, 52 times. Turning it into a smart report (Session 3) - owned, scheduled, validated - gave her Mondays back and removed the copy-paste errors nobody had been catching. Same output, but now a product.

Part 2 · the frame

Four tiers, and why you climb in order 7 min live

The 29 products are not a flat menu - they stack. Infrastructure stores and serves data. Analytics turns it into trustworthy answers. Data science predicts and personalizes. Gen AI governs and amplifies. Each tier stands on the one below. Skip a rung and the whole thing wobbles: a dashboard on unvalidated data is a confident lie, and an AI agent on a broken warehouse hallucinates with authority. This is the same lesson as Monica Rogati's AI Hierarchy of Needs - infrastructure before intelligence.

Self-studyWhy skipping a rung backfires2 min read▶

The most expensive data mistakes are all rung-skips. A few patterns you will recognize:

  • AI on a swamp - buying an "AI copilot" before the warehouse is clean. It answers fluently and wrongly, and trust never recovers.
  • Dashboards on sand - shipping 40 dashboards with no data validation. Every meeting becomes an argument about whose number is right.
  • Models with no home - a data scientist builds a great churn model that lives in a notebook and never ships. Value: zero.
The ladder is a sequencing tool, not a ranking Higher is not "better" - infrastructure is not lesser than AI. Higher just depends on lower. A mature org runs all four tiers at once; a growing one builds them in order.
Part 3 · the intuition

One question, answered at four maturities 5 min live

To feel the ladder, take one business question - "how are our stores doing?" - and watch the answer grow up as Kasa climbs. Same question, four very different products behind the answer.

Real world

Notice the gen-AI answer only works because everything under it is solid: the agent can "draft the board note and email the managers" only because the warehouse is trustworthy, the forecast is real, and governance says what it is allowed to send. Pull out any lower rung and the top answer becomes a confident fabrication. That dependency is the whole point of the course.

The running case

Meet Kasa, in full chaos ★ 8 min · everyone

Every session works on Kasa - a made-up but realistic Southeast Asian retailer we will grow up together. Meeting it messy makes the climb feel real.

The company. Kasa: ~4M customers, 180 stores across Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and the Philippines, a shopping app, and Kasa Pay - an embedded loyalty wallet with a small buy-now-pay-later line. Revenue ~US$1.2B, blended online and offline.

The chaos (where we start). Every team emails its own spreadsheet. Finance, marketing and store ops each have a different "revenue" number. The POS data is trapped per-store. Kasa Pay is bleeding to fraud that a hand-written rule list cannot keep up with. Support tickets pile up. Leadership wants "AI" but the warehouse does not exist yet.

The cast. Mei (new CDAO, our sponsor), Raj (data engineering), Sofia (BI + analytics), Deng (data science), the CFO (wants one trusted number), and the Kasa Pay risk team (wants the fraud bleeding to stop).

The journey. Sessions 2-5 build Kasa up one tier at a time; Session 6 shows how the 29 products stack into a single governed platform. Keep Kasa's chaos in mind - each product we meet is fixing a specific piece of it.

Kasa is fictional on purpose A synthetic company lets us be concrete without exposing any client. The numbers are illustrative, the problems are real, and the sequencing is exactly what Phoebe would recommend to a company at Kasa's stage.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What separates a data product from a one-off analysis?

The dividing word is "maintained" - a product has an owner, a refresh, and a way to break. A one-off answers once and dies in a slide.

2 · Why do you climb the ladder in order rather than skip to the top?

Higher is not better, it is dependent. An AI agent on a broken warehouse hallucinates with authority - the base has to be solid first. It echoes the AI Hierarchy of Needs: infrastructure before intelligence.

3 · The gen-AI answer "draft the board note and email the 12 managers" only works because...

The top of the ladder only acts correctly when every rung under it is solid. Remove the warehouse or the governance and the agent acts confidently on fiction.
