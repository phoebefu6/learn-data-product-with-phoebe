Part 1 · understand + group

Lift the people, then group the customers 17 min live

Before Kasa predicts anything, two things have to be true: the people reading the outputs must be able to question them, and the customers must stop being treated as one undifferentiated blob. The first product here, data literacy training, is honestly cross-cutting - it serves every tier. Phoebe places it in data science on purpose: it is the human on-ramp to the predictive tier, where confidence intervals, false-positive rates and model drift start to matter and a mean is no longer enough. The second, customer segmentation, is the gentlest form of "learning from data" - grouping the loyalty base so the right offer reaches the right person.

LiveData literacy trainingthe human on-ramp · 4 min▶
What it isA structured lift in the organization's ability to read, question, and use data - not a tool rollout, a capability rollout.
Kasa exampleStore managers learn to read a confidence interval, not just a mean; to ask "how fresh is this?" and "what is the sample?" before acting on a dashboard.
Business valueBetter decisions everywhere, and a prerequisite for both self-serve BI and safe AI - the tools only pay off if people can use them well.
Reach for it whenYour tools outrun your people's ability to use them - dashboards go unquestioned, or misread.
Tier🟠 Data Science but cross-cutting
Why literacy sits in the data science tier Be honest with your team about this one: data literacy is not only a data science product - it underwrites analytics, governance and gen AI too. Phoebe files it here because this is the rung where the stakes of misreading data climb sharply. Once you are acting on a forecast or a fraud score, "the average looks fine" is not good enough - people need to reason about uncertainty, base rates, and error costs. Literacy is the human on-ramp to the predictive tier.
LiveCustomer segmentation4 min▶
What it isGrouping customers by behavior and value so each group can be treated differently - the first honest step from "descriptive" toward "learned from data".
Kasa exampleRFM (recency, frequency, monetary) plus k-means on the loyalty base yields 6 actionable CRM segments - VIPs, regulars, at-risk, dormant, and two in between.
Business valueRight offer, right customer, higher ROI - marketing stops spraying one message at 4M people and starts spending where it converts.
Reach for it whenYou treat all customers the same - one newsletter, one discount, one journey - and returns are flattening.
Tier🟠 Data Science
Real world

Kasa was mailing the same 15%-off code to all 4M loyalty members every fortnight. Segmentation showed the VIPs did not need a discount (they buy anyway - the code just gave away margin), while the at-risk group needed a reason to return, not a coupon. Deng's 6 segments let marketing cut promo spend and lift redemption at the same time - the campaign that used to lose margin on VIPs now protects it, and win-back finally targets the people actually drifting away.

Part 2 · predict + productionize

Forecast the future, score it live, and keep it alive 20 min live

Now the tier earns its name. Sales forecasting turns history into a number about the future Kasa can buy against. Machine learning puts a model to work at scale - and its marquee use at Kasa is fraud detection on Kasa Pay, scoring every wallet and BNPL transaction faster than any hand-written rule list. The last card, data product, is the meta-lesson of the whole session: none of this is worth anything as a notebook. A model becomes a product only when it is deployed, owned, monitored, and retrained.

LiveSales forecasting4 min▶
What it isPredicting future demand or revenue from history plus drivers (seasonality, price, promotions, holidays) - a number about the future, with a stated uncertainty.
Kasa exampleA per store-SKU weekly demand forecast that feeds inventory buys across 180 stores - so the right stock lands in the right store before the week starts.
Business valueLess stockout (lost sales) and less dead stock (tied-up cash and markdowns) at the same time - the two costs a retailer pays for guessing.
Reach for it whenYou buy or plan against an unknown future and today rely on gut feel or last year's numbers.
Tier🟠 Data Science
Real world

Kasa's Jakarta stores kept selling out of a hero SKU by Thursday while the same item gathered dust in Manila. The forecast, cut per store-SKU rather than nationally, exposed the mismatch: demand was regional and Kasa was buying as if it were uniform. Rebalancing buys against the forecast cut stockouts on top sellers and shrank end-of-season markdowns - the same inventory budget, aimed better. A forecast with an honest confidence band also tells the buyer how much safety stock to hold, not just a point guess.

LiveMachine learning - fraud detection flagship★ 8 min▶
What it isModels that learn patterns from data to score, classify, or rank at scale - decisions made by a system, on millions of cases, faster and more consistently than people or fixed rules.
Kasa exampleFraud detection on Kasa Pay: a classifier scores each wallet and BNPL transaction for account-takeover, default risk, and promo-abuse - allow, hold, or deny in milliseconds. (The same toolkit also powers churn, propensity, and recommendations.)
Business valueBlock fraud without blocking good customers - a rule list either misses new attacks or freezes legitimate buyers; a model tuned on the cost of each error does both jobs better.
Reach for it whenThe hand-written rule list can no longer keep up with adversaries who change tactics faster than you can write rules.
Tier🟠 Data Science
Real world

Kasa Pay's fraud started as three problems at once: account-takeover (stolen logins draining wallets), BNPL default (buy-now-never-pay), and promo-abuse (rings farming sign-up credit with fake accounts). The old defense was a rule list the risk team edited by hand - and every time they patched one hole, the attackers found another. A single scored model reads device fingerprint, spend velocity, and account history together, catching combinations no single rule would. The decisive move was the loop: confirmed chargebacks and analyst labels flow back weekly to retrain, so Kasa Pay adapts as fast as its adversaries. Blocking fraud without freezing good customers is the whole game - and it is only winnable with a model that keeps learning.

The error you tune matters more than the model you pick A fraud model has two ways to be wrong: wave through a fraudster (false negative) or freeze a real customer (false positive). Those cost different amounts, so the "allow / hold / deny" thresholds are a business decision, not a technical default. Leaders should ask for the confusion matrix in money, not the accuracy score.
LiveData product - the meta-lesson4 min▶
What it isThe discipline that a model becomes a product only when it is deployed, owned, monitored, and retrained - not when it scores well in a notebook once.
Kasa exampleThe fraud score served live behind Kasa Pay checkout - with an owner (the risk team), monitoring on score drift and decision rates, and a scheduled retrain on fresh labels.
Business valueDurable value that compounds over years, versus a one-off model that impressed in a demo and then quietly rotted or never shipped at all.
Reach for it whenA model needs to run in production, reliably, for years - the moment it stops being a study and starts being infrastructure.
Tier🟠 Data Science
The four words, again Session 1 said a data product is built, owned, and maintained. This card is that lesson for models specifically. Deployed - it runs where the decision happens, not in a notebook. Owned - a named team is paged when it misbehaves. Monitored - someone watches for drift, not just launch-day accuracy. Retrained - it learns from new data on a schedule. A brilliant model missing any one of these is worth zero. This is where data science meets the engineering rigor of the lower tiers, and it is the bridge into the gen-AI tier next session, where agents act on data live and the same discipline gets even sharper teeth.
Before Session 5

This week ◐ 20 min total

  • Find one "treat everyone the same" moment. Where does your org send one message, one offer, or one journey to a very mixed audience? That is a segmentation opportunity - sketch the 3-4 groups you would split it into and the different action each deserves.
  • Name a forecast you make by gut. What do you buy, staff, or plan against an unknown future today? Estimate what a stockout and a dead-stock error each cost you - that gap is the value of a real forecast.
  • Audit your models for the four words. Pick a model your org has built. Is it deployed, owned, monitored, and retrained? Any "no" tells you it is a notebook, not a product - and where the value is leaking.
  • Optional: for a decision your org automates (fraud, credit, eligibility), write the cost of a false positive vs a false negative in money. Notice they are rarely equal - that asymmetry is the real setting on the model.
Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why is fraud detection built as a learning model with a feedback loop rather than a rule list?

A hand-written rule list is patched one hole at a time and always trails the attackers. A scored model reads many signals together, and the retraining loop - confirmed fraud feeding back - lets it adapt as fast as the fraud does. The loop is the product.

2 · What turns a model into a data product?

A brilliant model that lives in a notebook is worth zero. It earns the word "product" only when it runs where the decision happens, has an owner who gets paged, is watched for drift, and learns from new data on a schedule.

3 · Why does Phoebe place data literacy training in the data science tier even though it is cross-cutting?

Literacy underwrites every tier, but this is the rung where the stakes of misreading data climb sharply - acting on a forecast or a fraud score demands reasoning about confidence, base rates, and the cost of being wrong. It is the human on-ramp to prediction.
